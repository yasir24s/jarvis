# Voice spike: Kokoro-82M and F5-TTS in Swift

Measured on 1 October 2026 for D-48 and D-52, on this Mac: Apple M2, 8 GB, macOS 27.2
(26B5091g), Xcode 27.0, Swift 6.4. The spike is a standalone SwiftPM package at
`~/jarvis-spikes/voice-swift/`, outside the repository. Models, the reference clip and the
samples stay under `~/jarvis-spikes/`. This report is the only file from the spike in the
repo, and nothing was added to `native/Package.swift`.

## Summary

Kokoro was built and measured. F5-TTS was not: building it needs downloads outside the
approved list, so I stopped before fetching any of them (see "What failed").

- Kokoro-82M on CoreML through FluidAudio 0.17.4, voices `bm_george` and `bm_lewis`. Once
  warm, one sentence takes 0.085 to 0.30 s to synthesise. The p50 over 20 warm sentence
  calls is 0.147 s, against M8's budget of 600 ms. Real-time factor on whole lines is 0.044
  warm. Peak memory footprint is 538 to 571 MB per process. The first call after launch
  takes 1.2 to 1.7 s, so native needs a warm-up call at launch.
- F5-TTS on MLX (`f5-tts-swift`) is blocked. mlx-swift's Metal shaders build only with
  Xcode's Metal Toolchain, which is not installed here, and mlx-swift depends on
  `swift-numerics`. F5 also reads two small files that are not on the list.
- Recommendation: Kokoro through FluidAudio. Three things need settling first. FluidAudio's
  English frontend gives the British voices American phonemes, its G2P files live at a
  fixed `~/.cache` path, and its text normaliser is an 87 MB binary download that this
  spike left out.

## Method

Each run is one process per engine and voice, started by `measure.sh` under
`/usr/bin/time -l`. The process:

1. reads its own start time from the kernel (`sysctl`, `KERN_PROC_PID`);
2. creates `KokoroAneManager` and awaits `initialize(preloadVoices:)`. Cold start is the
   time from process start to that return;
3. synthesises each of the three lines whole, then each sentence of that line on its own,
   and then repeats the whole set (pass 1, then pass 2 as the warm repeat);
4. writes the pass-2 whole-line audio to `samples/` and one JSON record per run to
   `results/kokoro.jsonl`.

Time to first audio is the wall time of one `synthesizeDetailed` call. FluidAudio returns
an utterance's PCM in one piece, so the first PCM chunk is the whole sentence or line. The
sentence rows are what a sentence-by-sentence streamer would see. Real-time factor (RTF)
is synthesis time over audio duration. Memory figures come from `task_info(TASK_VM_INFO)`
(`phys_footprint`, `ledger_phys_footprint_peak`), `getrusage` and `time -l`. The 1-minute
load average was logged before every synthesis call and before and after every process.

State of the machine: no builds ran during measurement and memory pressure was at the
normal level at the start of every run, but swap already held 7.6 GB of 9.2 GB from the
rest of the session. The 1-minute load average sat between 2.8 and 4.0 from background
work; the busiest processes in a `ps` snapshot were Spotlight, the Claude app and MacPulse.
Run A was the exception: it started at 8.47, about a minute after a build finished.

Compute units were FluidAudio's default for the OS 27 line, `aneTailCpu`: five stages on
CPU and Neural Engine, the noise and tail stages on CPU only. FluidAudio's code notes
intermittent CoreML crashes on the 27 line (issues #843 and #889, reported on iOS). None
happened here in 112 synthesis calls across 7 processes.

### Phonemes

FluidAudio's English frontend loads only the US lexicon and the US G2P model, so the
British voices are fed American phonemes. Both voices got the same strings:


The `ɹ` in "sir" and the flapped `ɾ` in "battery" and "meeting" are American. I could not

as the intended words; the recogniser wrote "64%" for "sixty-four percent".

## The F5 reference clip

Prepared even though F5 could not run.

- `~/jarvis-spikes/voice-swift/clips/jarvis_ref_24k.wav`: 10.9 s, cut from 25.15 s to
  36.05 s of `~/jarvis/voices/jarvis_en.wav`. Mixed down to mono as (L+R)/2 and resampled
  to F5's 24 kHz with `AVAudioConverter`. Both ends fall in pauses (−40 to −58 dBFS in
  20 ms RMS windows).
- `~/jarvis-spikes/voice-swift/clips/jarvis_ref_24k.txt`: the transcript from Apple's
  `SpeechTranscriber`, en-GB, with the on-device model that was already installed (no
  download, and the tool never asked for speech-recognition permission): "Brilliant
  makeup artist called Veronica McAleer, who did my makeup and created that look,
  kneeling on the floor, whipping myself with a whip was peculiar."

A separate pass over the full 83 s file produced the same words for this stretch. I could
not listen to it, so the transcript is unchecked by ear and the spelling of the name is
unverified. An alternative cut, if this one sounds wrong, is 9.95 s to 23.30 s ("He
really understands actors. ..."), which ends on a trailing "and".

The full-file transcript shows that `jarvis_en.wav` is not dialogue from the films. It is
Paul Bettany talking about his own career in an interview: Brian Helgeland, A Knight's
Tale, Gangster No. 1, and a role in which he whipped himself. It reads as one speaker
throughout, and the pauses drop to −45 to −58 dBFS, which suggests there is no music bed.
Neither point is checked by ear. A clone from this clip would copy Bettany's own speaking
voice rather than the JARVIS delivery in the films. The brief calls it a clip of the movie
JARVIS, so the user should know before F5 is tried again.

## Streaming against M8's budget

M8's budget is p50 of at most 600 ms from sentence ready to first audio.

Kokoro streams sentence by sentence and meets it. Across the 40 sentence calls in the
clean runs (both passes), the p50 is 0.147 s and the slowest call 0.30 s. It does not
stream within a sentence: FluidAudio returns the sentence in one piece, and one call is
capped at 510 phonemes and 2000 acoustic frames, so long sentences must be split before
the call. The first call after launch (1.2 to 1.7 s) misses the budget unless native warms
the model at launch.

F5-TTS was not measured. It can only work sentence by sentence: each call generates the
whole sentence in one flow-matching pass conditioned on the 10.9 s reference, so every
call also processes the reference audio. Whether such a call fits in 600 ms on this Mac is
unknown.

## Recommendation

Use Kokoro-82M through FluidAudio. It is the only engine that was measured, it meets M8's
budget with room to spare once warm, and its samples transcribe back to the right words.
How native would carry it:

- Package: FluidAudio (Apache-2.0) as a SwiftPM dependency, pinned to 0.17.4, product
  `FluidAudio`, with `traits: []`. That is FluidAudio's own opt-out from its NeMo text
  normaliser, but the manifest still declares the 87.3 MB `NemoTextProcessing` binary
  target, and I did not test whether SwiftPM fetches it anyway. The spike used a local
  clone with that target deleted (seven lines of `Package@swift-6.2.swift`). Native needs
  the same patch, or the user's approval for the download. Without NeMo, English text goes
  through FluidAudio's Swift baseline normaliser. The three lines contain no digits, so
  that path was not exercised.
- Model files, in Application Support rather than the bundle: 51 files, 95.8 MB. The
  7-stage chain with three voice packs is 83.8 MB and the G2P model with its lexicon is
  12.0 MB. The chain accepts a `directory:` argument; the G2P files do not, because
  FluidAudio's `TtsCacheDirectory` fixes them at `~/.cache/fluidaudio/Models/kokoro` on
  macOS. Native needs a small FluidAudio patch there, or a symlink as the spike used.
  Native should fetch the files itself, pinned to the Hugging Face commit `006395f` and
  checked by size, so FluidAudio's own downloader never runs (see run A).
- Memory against the 8 GB limit: 0.33 GB once loaded, 0.43 to 0.49 GB between calls and
  0.54 to 0.57 GB at peak. That is about 1.5 times Python Piper's measured 356 MB peak and
  three to five and a half times the 100 to 180 MB the plan budgets for native Piper.
  Each fresh load measured here took 1.4 to 2.0 s to its first audio (0.2 to 0.3 s to
  load, then the 1.2 to 1.7 s first call), so unloading between turns would put that
  delay back on the next reply.
- Audio format: Kokoro returns 24 kHz float samples. M8's player expects s16le mono at
  22050 Hz (Piper's and ElevenLabs' rate), so it needs a second format or a resample.
- Accent: the American phonemes above. FluidAudio has a per-word override
  (`setEnglishCustomLexicon`), and misaki's British dictionaries (`gb_gold.json` and
  `gb_silver.json`, 6.5 MB together) are in the same Hugging Face repository, but
  FluidAudio does not load them. Not tried.

## What failed, and what fixing it needs

### F5-TTS: stopped before any unapproved download

Nothing for F5 was built. I read the manifests of the four approved packages,
shallow-cloned `f5-tts-swift` and `vocos-swift` (both approved, about 1 MB), and read the
88 KB header of `model_v1_8b.safetensors` with a range request. mlx-swift and the two
weight files were not downloaded, since nothing could use them yet.

To build and run F5, the spike needs these, none of them approved:

| Need | Source | Size | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metal Toolchain | Xcode 27 component: `xcodebuild -downloadComponent MetalToolchain` | not shown before download (not verified) | mlx-swift's README says command-line SwiftPM cannot build its Metal shaders and xcodebuild can. Here `xcrun metal` fails with "missing Metal Toolchain" and `xcodebuild -showComponent MetalToolchain` reports "uninstalled" |
| `swift-numerics` | github.com/apple/swift-numerics, Apache-2.0 | about 0.8 MB repository | mlx-swift's only dependency on macOS, for its `Complex` type |
| `vocab.txt` | huggingface.co/lucasnewman/f5-tts-mlx | 11 KB | F5's character vocabulary, read by `F5TTS.fromPretrained(modelDirectoryURL:)` |
| `config.json` | huggingface.co/lucasnewman/vocos-mel-24khz-mlx | under 1 KB | read by `Vocos.fromPretrained(modelDirectoryURL:)` |

Two more dependencies can be avoided with small local patches:

- `swift-transformers` 1.1.x (5.6 MB repository), which brings `swift-jinja` (0.3 MB) and
  `swift-collections` (16.9 MB). f5-tts-swift and vocos-swift import its `Hub` module only
  for `fromPretrained(repoId:)`. Both can also load from a local folder, so removing the
  import and those two functions drops it.
- `swift-argument-parser`, used only by f5-tts-swift's command-line target, which can go.

Patches that would still be needed after that:

- 8-bit weights. `model_v1_8b.safetensors` holds MLX-quantised layers: 8 bits, group size
  64, with `scales` and `biases` for every Linear layer. f5-tts-swift has no quantise step,
  so it needs a `quantize(model:groupSize:bits:)` call before loading.
- Versions. f5-tts-swift was last changed on 11 December 2024 and asks for mlx-swift 0.18
  or later. vocos-swift wants mlx-swift 0.29 or later and swift-transformers 1.1. mlx-swift
  HEAD carries MLX 0.32.0 and needs tools version 6.3. Whether f5-tts-swift compiles
  against it, and whether a port from December 2024 handles the v1 weights correctly, is
  not verified.

Disk: the data volume has 16 GB free (97 % used). The F5 set would add about 0.45 GB of
weights, plus the mlx-swift checkout and its build.

### Kokoro run A downloaded 42 MB by itself

This was my mistake. I put the four G2P files in `Models/kokoro-82m-coreml/`, but
FluidAudio looks for them in `Models/kokoro/`. When it did not find them, its downloader
fetched its G2P bulk set during run A: the same four files again, plus about 30 MB that
English does not use (`es_lexicon_cache.json`, `fr_lexicon_cache.json`, the `gb_` and
`us_` `gold` and `silver` dictionaries, `config.json` and `vocab_index.json`). All of it
came from huggingface.co/FluidInference/kokoro-82m-coreml, the approved repository. I
deleted the unused files and my misplaced copies. Every later run left the list of model
files unchanged. Run A's 34.3 s cold start includes that download.

Kokoro downloads in total: 100.1 MB fetched by me (51 files from a pinned commit, sizes
checked), about 42 MB fetched by FluidAudio, and a 21 MB shallow clone of FluidAudio. The
brief allowed about 325 MB for the model and about 288 MB for the clone. The 87.3 MB NeMo
xcframework was not downloaded.

### Smaller problems

- The first build failed within a minute because `SpeechTranscriber` needs a macOS 26
  deployment target. Raising the package's platform fixed it, and release builds then took
  80 s.
- Kokoro's first-launch time on a new machine is not separated from the download (see run
  F above).
- I could not listen to anything. The samples were checked by transcribing them on-device,
  and the reference cut by transcribing it twice.
